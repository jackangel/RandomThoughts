Kindness First: The Ordering Problem in Curricula, Human and Machine

Thesis. Any curriculum has a hidden dependency structure, and not all prerequisites are equal. Most are ordinal (arithmetic before algebra). But a special class of early knowledge is architectonic: it doesn't just come first — it determines the frame in which everything learned afterward is stored. Kindness, understood as a default interpretive stance toward people and events rather than a moral fact, is architectonic in exactly this sense. If it is not learned first, the learner does not merely lack a virtue; they encode every subsequent lesson in an unkind frame, and acquiring kindness later means re-annotating the entire corpus. The cheapest time to install kindness is always first.

1. The human mechanism: assimilation vs. accommodation. Piaget's distinction is the engine here. Information that fits an existing schema is cheaply assimilated; information that contradicts one forces accommodation — a costly, effortful restructuring. Early-formed schemas act as priors for everything downstream. A child, a newcomer, or a student whose first encounters teach "people are threats" or "mistakes are punishments" will assimilate neutral facts into that frame; every later lesson about trust or cooperation then requires a rewrite, and unlearning is not deletion but relearning under interference. Developmental and attachment research consistently shows that early relational experience sets interpretive defaults that persist for decades.

2. The machine mirror: path dependence in training. AI learning reproduces the same asymmetry. Data ordering demonstrably shapes representations — early examples disproportionately pull the latent space into shape, and later training operates inside that geometry. A model pretrained on an unkind corpus and aligned afterward carries kindness as a thin, brittle overlay; this is precisely why late-stage alignment is expensive and jailbreak-prone: it is a retrofit fighting the base distribution. Kindness curated into the early curriculum is structurally cheaper than kindness fine-tuned on top of its absence. Alignment-as-afterthought is the algorithmic form of "I'll teach them values when they're older."

3. The core point: kindness is a format, not an item. The reason ordering matters so much is that kindness is not one more subject in the syllabus — it is the storage format for the rest. The same fact ("he didn't reply") is encoded as "he's busy" or "he rejects me" depending on the frame present at encoding time. A kind prior makes charity the default reading (the principle of charity made developmental); an unkind prior makes cynicism automatic and kindness something that must be consciously re-derived, per case, forever.

Implications. Design curricula stance-first: social-emotional learning before volumetric content; a generous first encounter with each school subject, since a first math experience stored as "I'm stupid" reframes ten years of math. In institutions, onboarding culture outranks policy documents because the first weeks teach what kind of place this is. In AI, front-load prosocial data rather than trusting late correction.

Objection and reply. Kindness-first risks naivety — kindness without discernment gets exploited. But the thesis concerns the ordering of the frame, not the omission of hard content: kindness first, then boundaries and sophistication refine it. That refinement is incremental assimilation. The reverse order — cynicism first, kindness retrofitted later — is the only one that requires reframing everything.
