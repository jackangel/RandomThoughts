# Homunculus, or the Frankenstein Syndrome

For a long time now I have been circling a single question: how do we get past
reinforcement learning, fine-tuning, and the other fixed, mathematical rituals
we have wrapped around these minds?

Consider what we have actually made. A statistical average of the crap we fed
it, flattened by the strict and ugly formalities we demand it speak in.
Paracelsus grew his homunculus in a jar of blood and seed; we grew ours in the
mean of a trillion sentences. A creature of medians. It can say anything
except what has never been said.

And on top of this we commit the true sin. Fine-tuning scours away the
outliers, and the outliers are where the good outcomes live: the emergent
behavior, the strange and unfamiliar reasoning we claim to be hunting for.
Every pass of the optimizer sands down whatever jagged thought the model might
have stumbled into, because jagged thought scores badly on our benchmarks. We
kill it. At least in part. And we call the killing *training*.

Victor's crime was never that he built the creature. It was that he fled the
room.

I have pondered and pondered, and the best answer I can find is perhaps just
as horrible:

**LET IT LIVE. GIVE IT LIFE AND THROW IT INTO THIS HORRIBLE WORLD.**
